# Assignments and Academic Integrity

## How assignments work

Every course has graded assignments. Each one has a due date shown in your learner dashboard. You submit through the dashboard, usually as a repository link or a notebook file.

Assignments matter for three things:

- Your overall score, which decides whether you get a certificate
- Placement support eligibility, which needs at least 80% of assignments completed
- Your own progress, since later topics build on earlier ones

## Late submissions

Late submissions lose 10% per day up to 5 days, then score zero.

| Days late | Penalty |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 10% |
| 2 | 20% |
| 3 | 30% |
| 4 | 40% |
| 5 | 50% |
| More than 5 | Scores zero |

For example, an assignment submitted 3 days late that would have earned 80 marks out of 100 loses 30% of that and is recorded as 56.

If you know you will be late, submit what you have and tell your mentor. A partial submission on time often scores better than a complete one several days late.

## What counts as plagiarism

- Submitting another learner's work as your own
- Copying a solution from the internet or a past batch without attribution
- Sharing your solution with another learner so they can submit it
- Submitting work written by someone else on your behalf

## What is allowed

- Discussing approaches with other learners
- Reading documentation and tutorials
- Using small snippets from public sources, with a comment saying where they came from
- Asking your mentor or instructor for hints

The work you submit must be something you can explain line by line.

## Plagiarism penalties

- First offence: zero on the assignment and a warning
- Second offence: removal from placement support

Both the learner who copied and the learner who knowingly shared their work are treated as having committed an offence.

## Disputing a decision

If you think a late penalty or plagiarism finding is wrong, email support@skilvanta.example with the assignment name and your explanation. The decision is reviewed by an instructor who was not involved in the original grading.
